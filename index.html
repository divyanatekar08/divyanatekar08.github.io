About

I am a graduate student at New York University’s Center for Urban Science + Progress, pursuing an MS in Urban Data Science. My work focuses on building data-driven and AI-supported methods for understanding cities, including human mobility, retail behavior, environmental risk, spatial decision systems, and urban-scale machine learning workflows.

I am especially interested in how machine learning, geospatial data, large language models, and simulation-based approaches can support more explainable and actionable urban planning decisions.

Research

Thinking on the Move: Agent-Based Retail Simulation for Downtown Brooklyn

NYU CUSP + Downtown Brooklyn Partnership | 2025–2026

Developed an agent-based retail simulation framework for analyzing lunchtime mobility and restaurant choice behavior in Downtown Brooklyn. The project integrates mobility data, restaurant POIs, LLM-derived qualitative attributes, and spatial interaction models to support stakeholder-oriented retail planning.

Transient Object Removal from Urban LiDAR Point Clouds

NYU CUSP Summer Guided Research | 2025

Designed workflows for removing transient urban objects such as pedestrians and vehicles from LiDAR scans to improve the quality and consistency of 3D urban models.

Urban Wildfire Risk Prediction using Remote Sensing and Deep Learning

NYU CUSP | 2026

Built an urban wildfire risk prediction pipeline using MODIS satellite data, NASA FIRMS wildfire records, geospatial visualization, and deep learning workflows in Python and Google Colab.
