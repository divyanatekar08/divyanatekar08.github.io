About

I am a graduate student at New York University’s Center for Urban Science + Progress (CUSP), where I focus on building AI-driven urban analytics systems using geospatial data, human mobility patterns, machine learning, simulation models, and remote sensing pipelines.


My research explores how computational approaches such as agent-based modeling, spatial interaction models, large language models, and urban-scale data science can support explainable and actionable planning decisions for cities.


My work spans retail mobility simulation, wildfire prediction using satellite imagery, LiDAR point-cloud processing, urban visualization systems, and AI-enhanced geospatial workflows.

Research Experience

Thinking on the Move: Agent-Based Retail Simulation for Downtown Brooklyn

NYU CUSP + Downtown Brooklyn Partnership | Sept 2025 – May 2026

Developed an agent-based retail simulation framework for analyzing lunchtime mobility and restaurant choice behavior in Downtown Brooklyn.


Integrated gravity-based retail models, spatial interaction methods, large language models, and urban mobility datasets to model dining preferences beyond traditional distance-based assumptions.


Worked with restaurant review embeddings, mobility trajectories, and POI datasets to support stakeholder-oriented retail planning and intervention analysis.

Transient Object Removal from Urban LiDAR Point Clouds

NYU CUSP Summer Guided Research | Jun 2025 – Aug 2025

Designed machine learning and point-cloud filtering workflows for removing transient urban objects such as pedestrians and vehicles from LiDAR scans.


Improved fidelity and consistency of 3D urban models using spatial filtering, segmentation methods, and point-cloud processing techniques.


Presented research findings at the BATWorks Climate Event and the NYU CUSP Research Showcase.

Urban Wildfire Risk Prediction using Remote Sensing and Deep Learning

NYU CUSP | 2026

Built an urban wildfire prediction pipeline using MODIS satellite imagery, NASA FIRMS wildfire datasets, deep learning methodologies, and geospatial analysis.


Explored parameter-efficient fine-tuning approaches such as LoRA and PEFT for wildfire classification and urban environmental risk prediction tasks.
